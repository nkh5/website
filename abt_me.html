My name is Nicholas Hu, and I'm a senior at Rice University studying Electrical and Computer Engineering with a minor in Data Science.

I grew up outside Milwaukee and came to Houston for Rice. The question I keep coming back to is the one every hardware team eventually has to answer: how do you know it works? I like building the machinery that answers it, whether that's a register model generated from a single source of truth, a bench that sweeps a part until it breaks, or CI that catches the regression before a human does.

I've spent three summers at Rockwell Automation, which took me from motor drive firmware to production Kotlin to building hardware testbeds from a design spec. Most recently I traced a duplicate address validation defect in FactoryTalk Design Studio down to a topology accessor that resolved by name rather than by the device's actual topology, and rewrote it so every conflicting device reports its own error.

Alongside that I work on machine learning research with Rice ECE and Baylor College of Medicine, predicting depression severity from wearable and smartphone time series. We published in the Journal of Vascular Surgery in July 2026.

My side projects live where software meets silicon. I built an AXI4-Lite peripheral subsystem on an Artix-7, a silicon characterization bench that automates post silicon style validation across three microcontroller families, and GoldBench, a forecasting benchmark whose most useful finding was that the obvious signal isn't there.

On campus I lead the hardware team for OwlSat, Rice's CubeSat club, and sit on the IEEE officer board. Off the clock I'm probably reworking a board I already called done.
